OpenRocket automation

I wrote this optimizer over summer 2026 to compare the two-stage rocket’s motors, fins, body lengths, and ignition timing without editing and launching every simulation manually. I used OpenRocket as the flight solver, then handled the search, constraints, parallel execution, and result verification in Python.

RoleAerodynamic Simulations Team Lead
ToolsPython · JPype · OpenRocket · Multiprocessing · CSV / JSON

Screening motors before running trajectories

I first replaced an exhaustive 4,356-pair search with a thrust-curve screen. Total impulse alone does not tell me whether a booster will get the loaded rocket off the rail quickly, or whether a sustainer delivers its impulse over a useful part of the ascent. I calculated early thrust, rise time, peak timing, burn duration, and peak-to-average thrust from the actual curves.

The study retained 15 curves for each stage after the role filters and impulse ranking, giving 225 motor pairs. I kept each curve’s digest because different test curves can share a motor designation. That made the simulated curve, the recorded result, and the exported motor configuration traceable to the same data.

Rejecting unstable high-apogee cases

I ranked trajectories only after checking stability, rail-exit speed, and flight events. The August configuration required 1.5–4 calibers at launch, 1.5–6 through ascent after rail departure, at least 25 m/s off a 21 ft rail, and no tumble or simulation abort. These were the limits in that study’s configuration.

A motor pair that failed with the starting fins was not necessarily unusable. I added a fast static screen and up to two trajectory-based corrections: increasing aft-fin area and reducing canard area for insufficient stability, or making the opposite adjustment for excessive margin. The full flight still had to pass the configured checks after the correction.

Refining geometry and ignition delay

I sent the six leading qualified motor pairs into geometry and ignition-delay refinement. Fin area and position change the restoring moment, but also change drag and mass. Body-length changes affect both the package and mass distribution, so I checked the internal components and attachments as the dimensions changed.

Ignition delay adds another trade. Coasting can allow the rocket to climb into thinner air before the second burn, while a longer delay also reduces speed and changes stability at ignition. I swept the delay with the geometry because the best timing depends on the configuration flying that coast.

Running independent simulations in parallel

I used 12 spawned worker processes, each with its own OpenRocket JVM and temporary Java preferences directory. Keeping the Java runtime and model state separate prevents one candidate’s edits from carrying into another worker. The parent process handles ranking, checkpoint writes, and exports.

I saved the configuration, input-model hash, case records, and completed results so a run could resume without repeating finished cases. I also made changes to the search population explicit: a checkpoint from a 10 × 10 motor search cannot stand in for the additional pairs in a 15 × 15 search.

What the recorded run found

The saved run completed 475 screening simulations, 1,248 coarse geometry cases, and two refinement rounds of 936 and 287 cases: 2,946 simulations in total. Its best stability- and rail-qualified result reached 27,939 ft, with a 27.73 m/s rail-exit speed, a launch margin of 1.94 calibers, and an ascent margin ranging from 1.71 to 4.59 calibers.

That result was 2,061 ft below the 30,000 ft target. The output keeps the altitude-target failure separate from the passed stability, rail, and event checks. Checking that the exported model matches the result

I rebuilt the selected case from the unchanged source model, reopened the export, and checked its geometry, component masses, motor digests, staging events, launch settings, and saved flight summary. I then compared the rerun against the result that won the search. This catches an export that looks correct in the file browser but does not reproduce the configuration that was ranked.

The search finds the best case among the combinations it evaluates. It does not establish a global optimum, and the saved stability checks do not replace structural, flutter, or flight testing.
