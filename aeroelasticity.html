Hypersonic plate aeroelasticity

From January to June 2026, I studied a flexible cantilever plate in Mach 5.8 flow with Duke’s Aeroelasticity Group. I varied the plate thickness and analyzed its motion to investigate how structural stiffness changes the response to aerodynamic loading.

RoleResearch Assistant
ToolsANSYS · Transient CFD · Python · FFT

Simulation work

I varied thickness because it changes bending stiffness strongly: for the same material and plate width, bending stiffness scales with thickness cubed. Thickness also changes mass and natural frequency, so it affects how the plate motion couples with the unsteady flow. I ran transient cases to investigate conditions associated with limit-cycle oscillation, following the response over time rather than judging the plate from a steady load.

Reading the response

I used Python to read ANSYS time histories, resample unevenly spaced data where needed, and calculate FFT spectra. Uniform time spacing is needed for the FFT comparison; otherwise the sampling itself can distort the frequency result. I identified the dominant frequency and fitted a sinusoid to compare the frequency and amplitude between cases. The time history also shows whether an oscillation grows, decays, or approaches a repeated amplitude.
