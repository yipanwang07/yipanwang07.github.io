Sidepods and bodywork

I worked on the nosecone, bodywork around the electrical housing, and sidepod ducting for Duke Motorsports. I considered the internal cooling path and the external body shape together because both use the air arriving at the front of the car.

RoleAerodynamics Engineer / Team Lead
ToolsANSYS Fluent · SolidWorks · Composite layup

Sidepods and bodywork

For the sidepod ducting, I considered how the inlet and transitions deliver air to the radiator face. The core resists the flow, so the duct needs to preserve enough pressure to drive air through it. Sharp turns or abrupt expansions can separate the flow, increase pressure losses, and leave parts of the core poorly supplied.

That makes inlet area only one part of the problem. The path from the inlet to the core and the space for air to leave also affect the mass flow available for cooling.

External shape and packaging

I modeled the nosecone and bodywork around the electrical housing while working within the car’s component envelope. I also considered the outside of the sidepod: changing the duct or housing shape redirects air that continues toward the rear wing. I used the internal flow path and the surrounding aerodynamic package to guide the shape rather than judging the bodywork by frontal area alone.
