Duke AERO · 2025–present

Rocket geometry and loads

I compared nosecone profiles and worked through more than ten canard and fin iterations for Duke AERO. I used the CFD results to connect geometry changes to drag, control authority, and the loads that the fin structure has to carry.

RoleAerodynamic Simulations Team Lead
ToolsOpenRocket · RocketPy · RASAero · ANSYS Fluent

Geometry and loads

I compared nosecone profiles across speeds because the balance of skin-friction, pressure, and wave drag changes as the rocket accelerates. A profile that performs well at one speed may not give the best result across the ascent.

For fins and canards, I considered where the aerodynamic force acts as well as its magnitude. Increasing fin area can increase the restoring moment, but loading farther from the root also increases bending. I ran fin-loading cases to provide a basis for reinforcement and manufacturing decisions.

Mesh work

I refined the near-wall mesh because the body and control-surface loads depend on pressure and velocity gradients close to the surfaces. The reported y+ decreased from about 300 to 5, with a minimum orthogonal quality of 0.13 in the revised mesh. I treat these as mesh characteristics to assess alongside the wall treatment and force convergence.

Reading the pressure field

I used pressure contours to locate concentrated loads around the nose, deployed components, and fin roots. To obtain a resultant force or moment, the pressure has to be integrated over the surface with its direction and lever arm included. I used those solver results when passing loads into the structural work; the contour view shows where the loading comes from.
