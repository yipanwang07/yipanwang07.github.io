Composite manufacturing

I lead carbon-fiber wet layups for Duke Motorsports’ airfoils, side diffusers, and floor, and repair delaminated parts. My manufacturing work focuses on getting the intended surface shape and a sound composite part out of the tooling.

RoleAerodynamics Engineer / Team Lead
ToolsANSYS Fluent · SolidWorks · Composite layup

Composite manufacturing

The airfoils use machined clamshell molds to control both sides of the profile. Mold alignment and the leading-edge seam matter because a step or mismatch changes the surface the flow sees. I account for those features during the layup and assembly.

The construction combines carbon-fiber skins, foam cores, and printed trailing edges. Separating the skins with a light core gives bending stiffness without filling the section with solid laminate. The printed trailing-edge pieces define the thin geometry where forming and joining the skins is more difficult.

Layup and repairs

I have led wet layups for the wing surfaces, diffusers, and floor, as well as repairs to delaminated parts. In a layup, the fabric needs to follow the mold without bridging across corners or leaving poorly wetted regions. A delamination interrupts load transfer between layers, so a repair has to address the damaged laminate and its bond rather than only cover the visible surface.
